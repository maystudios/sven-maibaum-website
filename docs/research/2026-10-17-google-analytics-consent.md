# Google Analytics 4 and consent: vendor fact base (2026-10-17, not legal advice)

Date read: 2026-10-17 (all sources below, unless stated).
Method: each page was downloaded with `curl -sL` and a Chrome User-Agent, the visible text was extracted locally, and every quote was found with grep in that raw text. Google support pages were read in `?hl=en` and, where a German quote is given, `?hl=de`. Two JS-only pages (DPF list, Steam cookie settings) were read in a rendered Chrome tab; that counts as VERIFIED. WebFetch and web-search snippets were not used as evidence. Search results were only used to find URLs. VERIFIED = wording found in raw text or in a rendered page that I inspected. UNVERIFIED = see the list at the end.
Scope: GA4 with gtag.js, loaded only after the "Statistik" opt-in; Consent Mode v2 defaults denied, then analytics_storage granted before `config`; `allow_google_signals:false`, `allow_ad_personalization_signals:false`; on withdrawal `ga-disable-<ID>`, consent update denied, `_ga`/`_ga_*` deleted. Earlier facts: `2026-10-16-legal.md`.

## Findings

1. Contracting entity and Google's role. VERIFIED
- GA Terms, German country version, https://marketingplatform.google.com/about/analytics/terms/de/ ("Letzte Aktualisierung 11. Januar 2024"): "Diese Nutzungsbedingungen für Google Analytics (diese "Vereinbarung") gelten zwischen Google Ireland Limited (im Folgenden "Google") und der Rechtspersönlichkeit ..., die diese Vereinbarung unterzeichnet." The US version (`/terms/us/`) names "Google LLC". `/terms/ie/` returns 404.
- The same terms incorporate the processor terms: "Soweit Ihre Nutzung des Service umfasst ist, gelten die auf https://privacy.google.com/businesses/processorterms verfügbaren Google Auftragsdatenverarbeitungsbedingungen für Werbeprodukte". That URL now redirects to https://business.safety.google/adsprocessorterms/.
- The GA Terms also impose a disclosure duty on the site owner (§ 7): "Sie sind dazu verpflichtet, den Einsatz von Google Analytics offenzulegen und anzugeben, wie damit Daten erfasst und verarbeitet werden." They suggest linking to "www.google.com/policies/privacy/partners/". The same clause requires cookie information plus "dass sich der Nutzer damit einverstanden erklärt", where the law requires this.
- Google Ads Data Processing Terms, https://business.safety.google/adsprocessorterms/ : "“Google” means the Google Entity that is party to the Agreement." "“Google Entity” means Google LLC, Google Ireland Limited or any other Affiliate of Google LLC." Section 5.1.1: "(b) Google is a processor of Customer Personal Data"; "(c) Customer is a controller or processor, as applicable".
- Processor Services list, https://business.safety.google/adsservices/ : the list includes "Google Analytics". Data categories for Google Analytics: "Online identifiers, including cookie identifiers, internet protocol addresses and device identifiers; client identifiers".
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/3379636 (Data Processing Terms): customers "with businesses established in the EEA ... do not need to take such action as the Google Ads Data Processing Terms have already been incorporated in such customers’ terms". Also: "Amendments for new accounts are automatically accepted."
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/6004245 (Safeguarding your data): "Google operates as a data processor for Google Analytics." DE (`?hl=de`): "Google agiert als Datenauftragsverarbeiter für Google Analytics." and "Unsere Kunden sind dabei Datenverantwortliche".
- Address: no GA terms or processor-terms page gives Google Ireland's address. Google Privacy Policy (effective 16 Oct 2026, https://policies.google.com/privacy): "Google Ireland Limited for users of Google services based in the European Economic Area or Switzerland, located at Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland." DE: "Google Ireland Limited, mit Sitz in Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Irland". This sentence describes Google's consumer-side controller, but it is the same legal entity.

2. IP addresses (EU traffic). VERIFIED
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12017362 (EU, Switzerland, or UK-focused data and privacy): "When collecting data, Google Analytics does not log or store IP addresses from EU, Switzerland, or UK users." "For EU, Switzerland, or UK-based traffic, IP-address data is used solely for geo-location data derivation before being immediately discarded. It is not logged, accessible, or used for any additional use cases." "Google Analytics collects all data from EU, Switzerland, or UK-based devices (based on IP-geo lookup) through domains and on servers based in the EU, Switzerland, or UK before forwarding traffic to Analytics servers for processing." The derived fields are: City (and the city's latitude and longitude), Continent, Country, Region, Subcontinent.
- DE: "Bei der Datenerhebung in Google Analytics werden keine IP-Adressen von Nutzerinnen und Nutzern in der EU, der Schweiz oder dem Vereinigten Königreich protokolliert oder gespeichert." "Bei aus der EU, der Schweiz oder dem Vereinigten Königreich stammenden Zugriffen werden IP-Adressdaten nur zur Ableitung von Standortdaten verwendet und danach sofort gelöscht." (The DE text still says "über Domains und Server in der EU", without CH/UK.)
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/2763052 (legacy UA article): "In Google Analytics 4, IP masking is not necessary since IP addresses are not logged or stored." So `anonymize_ip` has no role in GA4.
- The IP address still reaches Google in transit. https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites says the browser "automatically sends certain information to Google. This includes the URL of the page you’re visiting and your IP address." https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9976101 says: "Note that Google Analytics does not store or log IP addresses."

3. Cookies. VERIFIED
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11397207 ([GA4] Cookie usage on websites): `_ga`, default expiration "2 years", "Used to distinguish users." `_ga_<container-id>`, "2 years", "Used to persist session state." DE: `_ga` "2 Jahre" "Dient zur Unterscheidung einzelner Nutzer."; `_ga_<container-id>` "2 Jahre" "Dient zur Speicherung des Sitzungsstatus."
- Same page: "The GA4 JavaScript tags use first-party cookies". It also says: "Browsers enforce limitations on the lifespan of first-party cookies if a user does not make a return visit (maximum of 400 days for Chrome and 7 days for Safari)." The admin override allows "from immediately to 25 months".
- Google ads cookie list (https://business.safety.google/adscookies/): `_ga` "Analytics" "Google Analytics" "2 years". `_ga_<wpid>` "Analytics" "2 years". Extra cookies (`_gac_*` etc.) apply only when linked to Google Ads (11397207: "When linked to a Google Ads account, GA4 JavaScript tags set additional cookies").
- Domain. GA4 config reference (https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/reference/config): "Set to 'auto' (the default value) to set the cookie to the top level domain plus one subdomain (eTLD +1)." The same page gives `cookie_expires` "default value time of two years (63072000 seconds)", and each hit extends that expiry. Customize-cookies guide (https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/guides/customize-cookies): "Cookies are set on the highest level of domain possible." `cookie_update` "true (the default value)".
- The old URLs `.../ga4/cookies-user-id` and `.../tag-platform/security/concepts/cookies` now land on a short overview page ("Cookies and user identification", last updated 2026-07-22). That page links to the guide above.

4. Data retention. Options and scope VERIFIED, default for a new property UNVERIFIED
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7667196 : user-level data can be set to "2 months" or "14 months". "This retention period also applies to key events data." Other event data: "2 months", "14 months", and 26/38/50 months for 360 only. "The two-month retention period is always applied to age, gender, and interest data regardless of your settings." "the data retention setting does not affect standard aggregated reports ... The data retention setting only affects explorations and funnel reports." "When data reaches the end of the retention period, it is deleted automatically on a monthly basis." The "Reset on new activity" option "applies to only user-level data".
- DE: "2 Monate" / "14 Monate"; "Die Einstellung für die Datenaufbewahrung hat keine Auswirkungen auf standardmäßige aggregierte Berichte ... Diese Einstellung wirkt sich nur auf explorative Datenanalysen und Trichterberichte aus."
- Google signals data: "The maximum amount of time that Analytics will retain Google-signals data is 26 months, regardless of your settings."
- None of the Google pages I read states the default for a new standard property: the help page, the GA360 limits page (11202874: "Up to 14 months, Options: 2, 14 months"), the Admin API DataRetentionSettings reference, or the GA4 Data Controls Guide PDF. Third-party blogs say 2 months; that is not used here.

5. Google signals. VERIFIED. The default state is supported by the sources but not stated in one sentence.
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9445345 : to activate, "Turn on the switch for Enable Google signals data collection." "When Google signals are enabled, reporting data is enriched for users who have turned on Ads Personalization and signed into their Google accounts, such as demographic and interest data." DE: "Stellen Sie den Schalter für Datenerhebung durch Google-Signale aktivieren auf „Ein“."
- Admin API (https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/config/admin/v1/rest/v1alpha/GoogleSignalsSettings): "Google Signals status defaults to GOOGLE_SIGNALS_STATE_UNSPECIFIED to represent that the user has not made an explicit choice." "Google Signals consent value defaults to GOOGLE_SIGNALS_CONSENT_UNSPECIFIED. This will be treated as GOOGLE_SIGNALS_CONSENT_NOT_CONSENTED." The GA4 Data Controls Guide PDF (linked from answer/13126616) says: "Google signals is an optional feature that can be enabled".
- Reading: signals collect nothing until someone switches them on in Admin. No page I read says this in one sentence.
- Tag field. Privacy guide (https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/guides/privacy): `allow_google_signals` "No effect when not set or set to true. When set to false, events sent from the tag will not be used for ads personalization and demographics and interests reports." Client-side effect: "When set to false, all join beacons are suppressed." Another sentence: "These configurations ... overrides any property settings established in the Google Analytics user interface." The config reference gives the default as `true`.
- Implementation note: Google's own gtag.js sample on that page is `gtag('set', {'allow_google_signals', false});`. That is not a valid object literal (comma instead of colon). Use `{allow_google_signals: false}`.

6. `allow_ad_personalization_signals`. VERIFIED
- Same privacy guide: "When set to false, events sent from the tag will not be used for ads personalization, but they can still be used for demographics and interests reporting." Client-side: "include an &npa=1 parameter on all beacons". Note: "ad personalization only happens when allow_ad_personalization_signals is set to true and the end-user grants consent to ad_personalization." The config reference gives the default as `true`.

7. `ga-disable`. VERIFIED
- https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/disable-analytics redirects to the privacy overview. The text is on https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/guides/privacy (section "Turn off Google Analytics"). Quote: "The gtag.js library includes a window['ga-disable-MEASUREMENT_ID'] property that, when set to true, turns off the Google tag from sending data. When a product attempts to set a cookie or send data back to the Google Analytics servers, it will first check if this property is set, and will take no action if the value is set to true."
- Caveat: "The window property must be set before any calls to gtag() are made, and it must be set on each page for which you want to turn off Analytics. If the property is not set or set to false, then Analytics will work as usual." The doc describes a check before each send. Setting the flag mid-session after earlier gtag calls is outside the documented order, and I did not test it.

8. Consent Mode v2. VERIFIED
- Consent types (https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/concepts/consent-mode): `ad_storage` "Enables storage, such as cookies (web) or device identifiers (apps), related to advertising."; `ad_user_data` "Sets consent for sending user data to Google for online advertising purposes."; `ad_personalization` "Sets consent for personalized advertising."; `analytics_storage` "Enables storage, such as cookies (web) or device identifiers (apps), related to analytics, for example, visit duration."
- Order (https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/guides/consent): "call the gtag('consent', 'default', ...) command on every page of your site before any commands that send measurement data (such as config or event)." Also: "By default, no consent mode values are set." and "Since consent mode doesn't save consent choices, update the consent status as soon as a user interacts with your consent management solution." gtag reference: "'default' is used to set the default consent parameters that should be used, and 'update' is used to update these parameters once a user indicates their consent."
- Basic mode (concept page): "you prevent Google tags from loading until a user interacts with a consent banner. This setup transmits no data to Google prior to user interaction with the consent banner." "when the user doesn't consent, no data is transferred to Google at all – not even the consent status." This site's design is basic mode.
- Advanced mode, with `analytics_storage` denied: "Won't read or write first-party analytics cookies or app identifiers. Measurements without 3rd-party cookies (web) ... will be sent to Google Analytics". Help page 9976101: "When analytics_storage='denied', cookieless pings are sent to Google Analytics. No Analytics cookies are set, accessed, or read from the device." "Cookieless pings ... may include the following information: user agent, screen resolution, IP address." DE: "Bei analytics_storage='denied' werden Pings ohne Cookies an Google Analytics gesendet."
- Relevance: after withdrawal the tag is still loaded in the open page. `consent update denied` alone would leave it in the advanced-mode state, which sends cookieless pings. Only `ga-disable` (finding 7) stops all sending. The design uses both.

9. Enhanced measurement. VERIFIED, except two sub-points
- https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9216061 : page_view fires "each time the page loads or the browser history state is changed by the active site". "An advanced setting on this option controls whether the event is sent based on browser-history events. This measurement option listens for pushState, popState, and replaceState." DE: "Dabei werden „pushState“, „popState“ und „replaceState“ erfasst." Other events: scroll (90 % depth), click (outbound links: link_classes, link_domain, link_id, link_url, outbound), view_search_results (URL params q, s, search, query, keyword), video_start/progress/complete (YouTube JS API), file_download (file_extension, file_name, link_classes, link_id, link_text, link_url).
- Form events. form_start fires on "the first time a user interacts with a form in a session" and sends `form_id` ("HTML id attribute of the <form> DOM element"), `form_name`, and `form_destination` ("URL to which the form is being submitted"). form_submit fires "when the user submits a form" and sends the same three plus `form_submit_text` ("text of the submit button, if present"). DE: "form_submit_text: Text der Schaltfläche zum Senden, falls vorhanden". No field-value parameter is listed. `form_length` is not listed on this page or on 9234069.
- Defaults (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9234069): page_view, scroll, click, file_download, form_start, form_submit, video_* and view_search_results are each marked "Collected by default via enhanced measurement." Page 9216061 adds: "By default, all event types are enabled" for automatic event detection in the Google tag.
- Also: "You're required to ensure that no personally identifiable information is collected." Data redaction (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/13544947): "When you create a new property, email data redaction is on by default." It covers `page_location`, `page_referrer`, `page_path`, `link_url`, `video_url` and `form_destination`.
- SPA guide (https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/single-page-applications): "Make sure to enable both Page loads and Page changes based on browser history events." The default of that sub-option, and whether a fragment-only change (`#anchor`) fires page_view, are not documented (see Unverified).

10. Automatically collected data. VERIFIED

It also says: "Geography dimensions (e.g., City, Region) are approximated based on the IP address of the traffic." Age, Gender and Interests appear there too and depend on Google signals.

## Implications for the privacy policy
1. Provider: Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Irland. It acts as Auftragsverarbeiter under the Google Ads Data Processing Terms, which are already part of the GA terms for EEA businesses. (1)
2. Transfer to the US: Google LLC is DPF-certified (EU-U.S., Swiss, UK Extension; active, next due date 13.09.2027). For analytics products Google relies on the EU-U.S. DPF since 1.9.2023, with SCCs as fallback. So the policy can name the adequacy decision (Art. 45 DSGVO) with DPF, and SCCs (Art. 46(2)(c)) as a secondary safeguard. (12)
3. IP address: it is transmitted technically. For EU traffic Google uses it only to derive coarse location (country, region, city) and then discards it; Google does not log or store it, and collection runs on EU servers. Do not claim "IP-Anonymisierung" (a UA feature). (2)
4. Cookies: `_ga` (distinguishes users) and `_ga_<ID>` (session state). Both are first-party, set on sven-maibaum.com, with a standard lifetime of 2 years from the last visit. Browsers may shorten this (e.g. Safari 7 days). They are set only after consent. (3, 8)
5. Data collected: page URL, referrer, page title, browser language, screen resolution, and the events page_view (including SPA route changes), scroll, outbound click, file download, form start/submit, video and site search. Derived from these: device category, browser, operating system and approximate location. The form events carry only the form's id/name, target URL and submit-button text, never field contents. Granular location/device data is collected by default unless switched off in Admin. (9, 10, 11)
6. Storage period: give the value actually selected in Admin (2 or 14 months for user- and event-level data). The default could not be verified, so the owner must read it from Admin > Data settings > Data retention before publishing. Aggregated standard reports are not affected by this setting. (4)
7. Google signals and ads personalization: switched off in the tag (`allow_google_signals:false`, which suppresses join beacons; `allow_ad_personalization_signals:false`, which adds `npa=1`). Consent Mode defaults deny all four types and only `analytics_storage` is granted. (5, 6, 8)
8. Consent and withdrawal: no Google request before the opt-in (basic Consent Mode). Withdrawal sets `ga-disable-<ID>` (Google: the tag then "will take no action"), sends a denied update, and deletes the cookies. The policy can offer the Google opt-out add-on as an additional option. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) DSGVO and § 25(1) TDDDG (see legal file). (7, 8, 13)
9. Required disclosures under the GA terms: disclose the use of GA and how data is processed, and link Google's partner-sites page. (1, 13)
10. LDI NRW block: Postfach 20 04 44, 40102 Düsseldorf; Kavalleriestr. 2-4, 40213 Düsseldorf; Tel. +49 211 38424-0; poststelle@ldi.nrw.de. Omit the fax (none published). (14)
11. Valve: Valve Corporation, P.O. Box 1688, Bellevue, WA 98009, USA; DPF and SCCs; EU representative RIVACY GmbH, Hamburg. The Steam widget sets at least `sessionid`. Valve's own list also names `steamCountry` (from an IP lookup) as always set on Steam sites. (14, 15)
Implementation notes (not policy text): delete `_ga`/`_ga_*` with `domain=.sven-maibaum.com; path=/`, because the `cookie_domain:'auto'` default sets them on eTLD+1 and a host-only delete would miss them. Do not copy Google's invalid `{'allow_google_signals', false}` sample. Keep `ga-disable` on withdrawal, because a denied update alone would leave a loaded tag sending cookieless pings. (3, 5, 7, 8)
